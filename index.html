How to study

The ancients didn't binge. They wove study into the day. Borrow their rhythm:

  • Morning (15–20 min): read one lesson. Read it twice — once for the shape, once for the detail.
  • Midday (10 min): do the lesson's Try exercise, then take its quiz. Application is the whole point.
  • Evening (5 min): answer the Check question from memory, flip a few flashcards in the Review room, mark the lesson done.
  • Each course's end: sit the final exam — 10 questions, 7 to pass. Then read the source texts in the Library that the course points to.

One lesson a day finishes the entire academy — 67 lessons — in just over two months. Your progress, quiz scores, notes, and streak are saved on this device automatically; nothing leaves your phone.

Why this order

Grammar before logic: you can't evaluate an argument you can't parse. Logic before rhetoric: persuasion without reasoning is manipulation. The tongues alongside: Latin and Greek unlock the sources themselves — start them whenever curiosity strikes. The quadrivium after the trivium: number needs a trained mind to mean anything. Philosophy last: ethics lands harder once you can read, reason, speak, and count. Gymnastics throughout: the Greeks trained the body alongside the mind from day one — don't save it for the end.
