Paideia · for the modern world

The New Academy

The classical Greek and Roman curriculum — grammar, logic, rhetoric, arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy, philosophy, and physical training — rebuilt as a self-paced course for a phone and a busy life.

What this is

For roughly two thousand years, educated people in the West studied the same core: the trivium (the three arts of language and thought) and the quadrivium (the four arts of number and the cosmos), crowned by philosophy and grounded in physical training. It produced people who could read carefully, reason cleanly, speak persuasively, count honestly, and live deliberately.

This site rebuilds that education lesson by lesson — no prior knowledge assumed, no Latin required on day one, every ancient idea paired with a modern use. Work the courses in order, or jump to whatever you need most.

How to study

The ancients didn't binge. They wove study into the day. Borrow their rhythm:

  • Morning (15–20 min): read one lesson. Read it twice — once for the shape, once for the detail.
  • Midday (10 min): do the lesson's Try exercise. Application is the whole point.
  • Evening (5 min): answer the Check question from memory, then look. Mark the lesson done.

One lesson a day finishes the entire academy — 54 lessons — in under two months. Your progress is saved on this device automatically; nothing leaves your phone.

Why this order

Grammar before logic: you can't evaluate an argument you can't parse. Logic before rhetoric: persuasion without reasoning is manipulation. The quadrivium after the trivium: number needs a trained mind to mean anything. Philosophy last: ethics lands harder once you can read, reason, speak, and count. Gymnastics throughout: the Greeks trained the body alongside the mind from day one — don't save it for the end.
